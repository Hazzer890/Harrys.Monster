What is FIRST?

Explainer Robotics education

The short version

FIRST is a not-for-profit that runs robotics competitions for school students. Dean Kamen, an American inventor, started it in 1989, and the name stands for For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology. The idea is to give engineering what school sport already has: teams, seasons, coaches and a crowd.

The programs

There are three, and a student can move up through them.

  • FIRST LEGO League (FLL). Primary and lower secondary students build and program LEGO robots to run missions on a table, and present a research project alongside.
  • FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC). Ages 12 to 18. Teams build a robot that starts inside a 45 cm cube and play two against two on a 3.6 m square field.
  • FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC). Ages 14 to 18. Robots of up to 60 kg, built in about two months. I've written a separate page on how FRC works.

Where I fit

I started as an FRC student in 2016 and I've been lead mentor of FRC Team 4788 at Curtin since 2021. I've been a head referee for FLL, and this year I'm also mentoring Lebob, an independent FTC and FLL team in Perth. Robotics West, which I chair, was set up to give more WA students access to programs like these.

Words you'll see on this site

  • Gracious Professionalism. FIRST's term for competing hard while treating the other teams well. At an event teams lend each other parts and tools, including to the team they're about to play.
  • FIRST Impact Award. The top award in FRC, for the team that has done the most for its community and for other teams over several years. It was called the Chairman's Award until 2023.
  • Engineering Inspiration Award. For getting a team's school and community interested in engineering.
  • Dean's List. An award for individual students, for leadership and technical work, named after Dean Kamen.
  • Woodie Flowers Award. The award for mentors. Students write the nomination, and it's named after the MIT professor who helped start the competition.
  • Regional. A three-day FRC event. Winning one, or winning the Impact or Engineering Inspiration award there, qualifies a team for the Championship.
  • Championship. The world final, held in Houston each April.
